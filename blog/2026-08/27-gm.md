## Chess Review

To be honest, I'm having hard time fully understanding chess. Right now it is just 'play and bind pattern recognition to your brain'. When I watch a video or a review of a game of 1000+ it is most basic advices of 'get your pieces out on the board'.
I watch something of 2500 and they just say: make sure your moves are doing those 3 things!

Which is very very basic advices to be honest. It is turned-based game with a limited board, so it makes sense that number 1 priority is to maximize the value of a move. And in order to do that, move should be multi-purposed... in order to be multi-purposed your pieces have to be active on the board and influence as much squares as possible, thus heavy need for early development of pieces.
Then it is a question about seeing those moves, but it is about pattern recognition -> calculations. Heavy trained pattern recognition leads into intuitive understanding of a possible move chain. Ex. Having pawns on dark squares make white bishop and queen into a menace, because now they can abuse undefended squares much better. Or recognition that rook is stuck and how it gives ability to ignore its existence. Or even sacrificing pieces to gain pressure on the king.

My inner problem comes later: it feels like a 'solved' game. Meaning it is a very objective game with objective moves... so you are not winning, you just doing your best not to lose. Then who makes more mistakes - loses. So it very feels like a game where you not really trying to win at all: you do a set play -> shuffle -> gotcha...

Maybe it is what is called 'patience'. But Blitz chess is popular for a reason, so I feel like I'm not the only one... Also Fisher was saying back in the days that chess is not fun after openings got solved.
