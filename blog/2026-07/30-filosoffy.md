Kind of just a topic mostly on philosophy of 'status'. Emergent invention of nature. Problem with that is: people tend to think that it is something what our brave minds and hearts figured out. But if you take a bunch of sand, take your gentle 2 hands and push it closer together, you will make a hill. Naturally some grains of sand will end up on the very top.

That's your King!

Most deserving? The hardest worker? The one who can properly lead the 'sand kingdom'?... no, just emergent.
Let's give sand grains a mind to figure it out. Eventually there will be the MOST capable grain of sand on the top of the hill, right?

It becomes a gameplay of 'get on the top of the hill', rather than a philosophy of 'rule'. Then interestingly enough a group of 'like-minded' will naturally form and realise: King is one, we can't be all King, so, let's be the ones to decide what King shall do. So we are true kings without crowns and when King is blamed for things, he is the perfect scapegoat as well.

And again, gameplay is not about philosophy of proper 'rule', but of 'how to split and maintain the King'... It becomes a 'game' problem, while players are just 'playing' and optimising strategies.

Solution is actually simple: population itself should be of enough clarity with understanding of underlying philosophy of the 'rule'. So when King emerges, it will emerge from that 'population', rather than be the one with the most 'schemes' under the belt.
Even though it is simple: it requires the whole population to be inline with themselves. Utopia.

History also doesn't help. If we actually learned mistakes from our past, there will be no wars. Doesn't happen. History becomes 'what type of war we didn't try yet, so maybe this time... it will be different!'... The whole idea of being 'smarter', than previous generation leads into that trap all the time.

So we are left with the whole 'philosophy of rule' as the last resort.

## Philosophy

Even though it is a big term that has the same stature as a 'King', it is 'Appeal to Ancient Wisdom' fallacy giving you Plato + Greek + Latin feel with perceived 'authority' behind it... which is kind of a giant paradox of its own: we are smarter now! BUT! When 'past' goes far back enough = those were actually smart people! And then we proceed to say how stupid of them painting a 'dragon', when they saw a lightning strike... 

Overall: Philosophy is a way of resolving 'fluid' questions within ones mind. [Fluid means there is a gradient, not a yes / no state. Your answer lands on a spectrum, not on the point]

And resolution of fluid questions via 'philosophical thought' leads to 'confidence' and proper build of foundational logic. For example: 'what is a good day?'. If a person cannot answer it within their own subjective mind... how tf you are on the throne?...

But even things like: 'why I buy 2 bottles of milk, instead of 1?' is a good question that will lead into a lot of introspections about 'self'. The more of those questions are resolved the more confidence it will 'manifest' and act as a proper fundament for deeper / higher questions.

Within our 'age of knowledge' and hunt for objectivity, we did lose a lot of 'subjective truths'. And even with our 'objective' reality we state that being alive and conscious = having subjective experience. That's makes you wonder... how much 'knowledge' and 'set truths' manifest an 'unconscious' state within one self.

And the main problem of 'unresolved subjective perspectives' lead to instant doubt, that could be exploited by entities who do provide a 'concrete answer' on the spot. Let's say: 'what is a good citizen?' 'the one who respects the king of course!'... read it a few times and see how flawed it is. You may start to refine it with 'the one who abides the law', but then 'who makes the law?', maybe 'citizen who doesn't cause trouble'... for who? 'for other citizens of course'. How do we know who is truly troubled and who cries wolf? Maybe 'the one who helps other citizens!' but what is 'help'?... see... Фilosoffy!
